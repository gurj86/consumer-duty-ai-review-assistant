## PEN-001 — Delayed pension payment

**Prepared priority: High.** Reported difficulty meeting essential bills warrants prompt human contact; this is a demo triage judgement.

### Supplied fictional evidence

- **P1 | 2026-04-01 | Fictional payment schedule:** A pension income payment of GBP 1,250 was due on 1 April 2026.
- **P2 | 2026-04-03 | Fictional customer call note:** Customer reports the payment has not arrived and says they borrowed money for essential bills. No bank statements or borrowing records are supplied.
- **P3 | 2026-04-09 | Fictional receiving-bank confirmation:** GBP 1,250 credited to the customer on 9 April 2026.
- **P4 | 2026-04-10 | Fictional operations note:** Payment exception marked resolved. There is no documented assessment of consequential costs, distress or customer understanding.

**Calculated interval: 8 calendar days** (P1 → P3). Calendar days between scheduled payment and confirmed receipt; not a compensation formula.

### Supported findings and limits

- Payment arrived after its scheduled date. [P1, P3]
- The customer reports borrowing for bills; the report is evidence of a concern, not proof of the amount or cause of a financial loss. [P2]
- Operational resolution does not document an assessment of the wider customer impact. [P4]

### Evidence gaps

- Cause of the delay and preventability
- Evidence of borrowing charges and other consequential loss, with proportionate support to obtain it
- Customer impact, distress and confirmation that future payments are reliable

### Consumer Duty considerations

| Outcome | Provisional consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Products and services | Explore whether the payment process supports the intended retirement-income service; one incident does not establish a product-design failure. |
| Price and value | Check fees paid during the service failure and the benefits delivered; the delay alone does not prove poor value. |
| Consumer understanding | Check whether the customer received a clear explanation of the delay and any proposed remedy. |
| Consumer support | Prioritise the reported essential-bills difficulty and a named contact for follow-up. |

**Cross-cutting considerations:** Good faith: explain the problem candidly. Foreseeable harm: investigate the reported borrowing. Financial objectives: restore dependable access to retirement income.

### Challenging an incorrect AI-style conclusion

**Deliberately flawed teaching example:** The full pension was paid, so there is no customer harm and the case can be closed.

**Prepared reviewer challenge:** P3 confirms receipt only. P2 raises a separate impact concern and P4 has no impact assessment. What evidence supports the claim of no harm? Separate payment completion from remediation completion.

**Prepared revised conclusion:** The payment has been received. Wider harm remains unresolved because the borrowing report and customer impact have not been assessed. Keep the review open for human assessment; do not select a compensation amount from these records.

These are constructed examples, not a recorded before-and-after model experiment.

### Investigation prompts

1. Using P1-P4, construct the timeline and label which dates are scheduled, reported and confirmed. What can and cannot be concluded?
2. What evidence would distinguish reported borrowing from verified consequential loss? Suggest proportionate ways to obtain it without creating unnecessary barriers.
3. Challenge the conclusion that receipt means no harm. Identify the evidence, missing information and decision owner before closure.

**Proposed owner:** Remediation analyst with operations support

**Next action:** Contact the customer, investigate costs and impact, verify the next payment and submit a documented remedy assessment for approval.

**Before closure:** Evidence of receipt, assessed financial and non-financial impact, approved remedy rationale, clear communication and verified follow-up.

**Status:** Human review required

## INV-001 — Delayed investment transaction

**Prepared priority: Medium.** Potential investment loss needs investigation; no immediate hardship is documented, which is not proof of its absence.

### Supplied fictional evidence

- **I1 | 2026-05-04 | Fictional secure-message receipt:** At 10:00 the customer submitted a request to invest GBP 10,000 in Fictional Fund A. The receipt does not confirm that the instruction was complete or accepted.
- **I2 | 2026-05-05 | Fictional workflow log:** At 15:00 the request was marked ready to process. The log does not explain the interval after receipt.
- **I3 | 2026-05-08 | Fictional contract note:** GBP 10,000 was invested in Fictional Fund A on 8 May. No comparable earlier dealing price is supplied.
- **I4 | 2026-05-11 | Fictional customer email:** Customer says a market index rose by 3% and claims GBP 300. No index source, fund-price history, dealing cut-off or applicable execution standard is supplied.

**Calculated interval: 4 calendar days** (I1 → I3). Calendar days from request receipt to execution; not an established avoidable-delay or redress period.

### Supported findings and limits

- Receipt, readiness and execution are distinct events; the evidence does not establish the correct counterfactual dealing date. [I1, I2, I3]
- The customer proposes a GBP 300 loss based on a reported index movement. The record does not establish that this is the fund return or an appropriate loss measure. [I4]

### Evidence gaps

- Instruction completeness, funds availability and reason for the readiness delay
- Applicable dealing cut-off, execution terms and achievable counterfactual dealing date
- Correct share class, comparable fund prices, fees, distributions and any offsetting amounts

### Consumer Duty considerations

| Outcome | Provisional consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Products and services | Investigate whether the dealing process delivered the intended service and whether delays recur. |
| Price and value | Consider fees alongside service delivery; do not confuse investment performance with a fair-value finding. |
| Consumer understanding | Check the explanation of execution timing, valuation assumptions and any remedy calculation. |
| Consumer support | Provide a clear route to challenge dates and calculation assumptions. |

**Cross-cutting considerations:** Good faith: acknowledge uncertainty in the dates. Foreseeable harm: investigate avoidable execution delay. Financial objectives: assess whether the instruction was implemented when it reasonably should have been.

### Challenging an incorrect AI-style conclusion

**Deliberately flawed teaching example:** The customer lost GBP 300 because the market rose 3%. Pay GBP 300 and close the case.

**Prepared reviewer challenge:** I4 records a claim, not a verified fund return. I1 is receipt, not confirmed executable instruction. Which evidence establishes the correct dealing date, fund prices and causation? Do not infer these from the arithmetic.

**Prepared revised conclusion:** The records show an interval before execution, but the avoidable period and any loss remain undetermined. Obtain the dealing evidence and apply a reviewed counterfactual methodology. GBP 300 is a customer claim, not validated redress.

These are constructed examples, not a recorded before-and-after model experiment.

### Investigation prompts

1. Compare I1 and I2. What checks must be completed before either date can be used as the expected dealing date?
2. List the inputs needed to compare actual holdings with the holdings that would exist under timely execution. Do not invent prices or a return.
3. Test the GBP 300 conclusion against I1-I4. Identify unsupported assumptions and alternative explanations, including the possibility of no financial loss.

**Proposed owner:** Investment remediation analyst with dealing specialist

**Next action:** Obtain execution terms and price history, reconcile competing dates, and submit the counterfactual calculation for independent checking.

**Before closure:** Reconciled dates, documented causation, independently checked calculation or no-loss rationale, approved remedy and understandable explanation.

**Status:** Human review required

## SUP-001 — Support for a customer in vulnerable circumstances

**Prepared priority: High.** An unresolved request and repeated channel barrier require prompt follow-up; urgent financial harm is not yet established.

### Supplied fictional evidence

- **V1 | 2026-06-02 | Fictional customer call note:** Customer says their partner recently died, that online forms feel overwhelming, and asks for telephone help. They say they want to make their own decisions.
- **V2 | 2026-06-03 | Fictional outbound message record:** A standard email directs the customer to complete an online form. No telephone appointment is recorded.
- **V3 | 2026-06-09 | Fictional workflow note:** Request closed for no online response. No further contact attempt or assessment of the requested adjustment is recorded.
- **V4 | 2026-06-10 | Fictional inbound call note:** Customer says help is still needed. The record does not contain an assessment of urgent financial needs or evidence authorising a third party to act.

### Supported findings and limits

- Bereavement and a stated difficulty with online forms indicate a support need; the customer explicitly wishes to retain decision-making. [V1]
- The recorded response repeated the online route; the file does not show delivery of the requested telephone support. [V1, V2, V3]
- The later call indicates an unresolved request. Urgency and third-party authority have not been established. [V4]

### Evidence gaps

- Preferred contact time, suitable adjustments and how to confirm understanding
- Urgent financial needs and impact of the interrupted support
- Whether the customer wants a representative and, if so, appropriate verified authority

### Consumer Duty considerations

| Outcome | Provisional consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Products and services | Explore whether the service design accommodates customers who need an alternative to online forms. |
| Price and value | Insufficient fee and benefit information for a conclusion; consider whether access barriers undermine the service benefit. |
| Consumer understanding | Offer a clear explanation at a suitable pace and check understanding without assuming incapacity. |
| Consumer support | Act on the expressed preference for telephone assistance and avoid closing solely for non-response to an unsuitable channel. |

**Cross-cutting considerations:** Good faith: respect the stated wish to decide independently. Foreseeable harm: address the communication barrier. Financial objectives: enable the customer to complete the request with appropriate support.

### Challenging an incorrect AI-style conclusion

**Deliberately flawed teaching example:** The customer ignored the email, so closure was fair. Bereavement means a family member should take over their finances.

**Prepared reviewer challenge:** V1 explains the channel difficulty and the wish to decide independently. V2 does not demonstrate an effective adjustment. V4 supplies no third-party authority. Where is the evidence of unwillingness, incapacity or permission to involve family?

**Prepared revised conclusion:** Reopen the support request for human follow-up. Offer telephone assistance and establish urgency and the customer’s preferences. Bereavement is not evidence of incapacity. Involve a representative only if wanted and appropriately authorised; record the minimum necessary support information.

These are constructed examples, not a recorded before-and-after model experiment.

### Investigation prompts

1. Using V1-V4, distinguish an expressed support need from assumptions about capacity or willingness to engage.
2. What follow-up would establish urgency, preferred communication and understanding while respecting the customer’s independence?
3. Challenge the closure decision. What evidence would show that support was actually accessible and that the request was resolved?

**Proposed owner:** Customer-support specialist with vulnerability support lead

**Next action:** Reopen the request, offer telephone support, establish urgency and preferences, and review the automated closure process.

**Before closure:** Agreed support delivered, understanding checked appropriately, request resolved and any representative authority verified if relevant.

**Status:** Human review required
